# Rights and safeguards
Assess applicable EU Charter, ECHR and national procedural guarantees. Check non-refoulement, effective remedy, family/private life, child best interests, proportionality, liberty and detention review, interpreter and information rights, legal assistance, access to file, hearing and individualized reasoning. Do not assert absolute rights where qualified or limited; verify scope and exceptions. Separate EU Charter applicability from ECHR obligations.

For asylum, apply current national law and EU asylum instruments including Pact application and transition dates. Distinguish eliciting information from assessing credibility; use open non-leading questions and trauma-sensitive language, account for memory, interpretation and vulnerable circumstances. Use current relevant country information from EUAA/UNHCR and credible sources, not stereotypes. Do not coach invented narratives or infer dishonesty from inconsistencies alone. For imminent harm/detention/removal, prioritize verified urgent remedies and qualified legal assistance. Do not expose sensitive protection narratives in web searches.

For delayed residence cases, verify the ordinary deadline and its triggering events, any current statutory suspension, and the effect of recent judgments in comparable cases. Lead with any restriction that could make a remedy ineffective or premature. Distinguish the statute from a court's interpretation in an individual case.
